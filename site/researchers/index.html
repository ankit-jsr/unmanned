The resubmission tax no one talks about

You spent months on the paper. The science is done. Then the reviews come back and the response letter becomes its own project — matching reviewer numbering, calibrating tone, deciding how firmly to push back on comment 3 from Reviewer 2.

The letter is a known format. Once you've decided how to handle each comment, turning those decisions into a numbered, professional, point-by-point letter is exactly what a model is good at.

What the skill does

  1. Paste the reviews, your paper summary and your decisions The reviewer reports, labelled by reviewer, and the paper title and abstract. For each comment, say whether you accept it, partly accept it or push back, and what you changed.
  2. Every comment gets a number, a classification, and a draft response Accept (A), Partial (B) or Pushback (C), following your decisions. Each response says what you changed and where. It's built to use only the details you give it and to bracket the rest, including any decision you haven't made. Check every specific before you send.
  3. Get a paste-ready letter Opening paragraph, full point-by-point responses, summary of major changes. Ready to paste into your submission system.
Example — urban tree canopy paper, Urban Climate. [Brackets] mark details you supply.

Comment R1.1: "The methods section does not explain how 'matched controls' were selected. This is a critical omission."

A — Accept Response: We thank the reviewer for identifying this gap. We have added a paragraph to [section] describing how matched controls were selected: [your matching criteria and method].

Comment R2.1: "The conclusion section reads as if causation is established."

A — Accept Response: We agree that our design shows association, not causation. We have revised the conclusion ([where]) to replace causal language with associational language, and added [the caveat you wrote about causal inference].

It handles the hard cases too

Internally contradictory reviewer comment? The skill flags the contradiction and asks you how to handle it before drafting — rather than guessing and getting it wrong.

Reviewer demanding you change a scientific conclusion you disagree with? The skill drafts a firm pushback from the justification you provide, in a collegial tone.

Missing the reviewer comments? It asks for them before proceeding — it won't invent them.
