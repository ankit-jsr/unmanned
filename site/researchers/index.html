The resubmission tax no one talks about

You spent months on the paper. The science is done. Then the reviews come back and you spend another week on the response letter — matching reviewer numbering, calibrating tone, deciding how firmly to push back on comment 3 from Reviewer 2.

The letter is a known format. The classification — accept, partially accept, respectfully disagree — follows a clear logic. The professional language is a template. This is exactly what a model is good at, and exactly what most researchers are spending hours on.

What the skill does

  1. Paste your reviewer comments and paper summary The full text of the reviewer reports, labelled by reviewer. Plus the paper title and abstract (or a brief summary of your argument).
  2. Every comment gets a number, a classification, and a draft response Accept (A), Partial (B), or Respectful pushback (C) — with a 2–4 sentence response for each that says exactly what was changed and where.
  3. Get a paste-ready letter Opening paragraph, full point-by-point responses, summary of major changes. Ready to paste into your submission system.
Example — urban tree canopy paper, Urban Climate

Comment R1.1: "The methods section does not explain how 'matched controls' were selected. This is a critical omission."

A — Accept Response: We thank the reviewer for identifying this gap. We have added a new paragraph in Section 2.3 describing the matching criteria in full: neighbourhoods were matched on baseline canopy cover (±2%), impervious surface fraction (±5%), median household income (±10%), and population density (±15%), using nearest-neighbour propensity score matching. Standardised mean differences before and after matching are reported in Supplementary Table S2.

Comment R2.1: "The conclusion section reads as if causation is established."

A — Accept Response: We have revised Section 5 (paragraphs 1 and 3) to replace causal language with associational language throughout. For example, "tree canopy expansion reduces temperatures" is now "tree canopy expansion is associated with lower surface temperatures." We have also added a sentence noting that the design limits confounding but does not establish causation.

It handles the hard cases too

Internally contradictory reviewer comment? The skill flags the contradiction and asks you how to handle it before drafting — rather than guessing and getting it wrong.

Reviewer demanding you change a scientific conclusion you disagree with? The skill drafts a firm, evidence-based pushback that cites your methodology and maintains a collegial tone.

Missing the reviewer comments? It asks for them before proceeding — it won't invent them.
