The problem every grant writer knows

A full proposal takes days, and too much of that time goes to bad-fit opportunities. The funder wants rural health programmes; your org is urban. The funder expects a track record in direct service; yours is in capacity building. You find out at the debrief, six months later.

Fit assessment should take 20 minutes, not be skipped entirely because there is no time.

What the skill does

  1. Paste the RFP and your org profile The key sections: eligibility, priorities, award range. Plus your mission, geography, budget, track record.
  2. Get a structured fit memo in under a minute Mission alignment, eligibility, and capacity — each scored Strong / Partial / Weak with a one-line reason.
  3. Act on the verdict immediately High: a list of the 3 requirements most likely to trip up a first draft. Medium: 2–3 concrete actions to close the gap. Low: the specific blocker, stated clearly, so you can move on.
Example output — Metro Housing Stability Fund
DimensionScoreReason
Mission alignmentPartialWorkforce development org; clients face housing risk but primary mission is employment.
EligibilityStrongDenver metro, $400K budget, 7 years operating — all criteria met.
CapacityPartialOne pilot financial coaching workshop. Funder expects demonstrated housing programming.

Verdict: Medium — secure a letter of support from your housing nonprofit partner and pull client data showing housing instability outcomes before you apply.
