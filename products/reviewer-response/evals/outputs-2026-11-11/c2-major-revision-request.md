## Two things to check before you choose a response

1. **Which way the bias goes.** The reviewer says that because parents underestimate exposure by 30-40%, your effect sizes are "likely underestimates". That only holds under some assumptions about the error. Suppose every parent under-reports by a similar proportion. Then the effect per reported hour could be *over*stated rather than understated. Your >3 h/day cut-off adds another twist: under-reporting would put some truly high-exposure children in the low group, which usually shrinks the difference between groups. Do you agree with the reviewer's direction? Or do you want to say that the direction depends on the error structure? The response to R1.1 changes depending on your answer, so I've left it open.
2. **Whether the two remedies fit the concern.** Option (b) asks you to reframe a cohort of 847 children followed for 3.5 years as a "pilot study". That label usually means a small feasibility study, so it doesn't follow from a measurement concern. Option (a) asks for device logs, but your exposure window was ages 2-3 and the children are now 5 or older. Can you still collect device logs that validate the diary for that window, for example from a new or ongoing sample? Or would the validation only be indirect? Please tell me which option you're taking, if either, and what you can actually do.
