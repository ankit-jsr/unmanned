# Grant Fit Screener

## When to use

When a nonprofit grant writer has received or found a funding opportunity and needs a fast,
honest assessment of whether the organisation should invest 10–80 hours writing a full
proposal. Run this before any drafting begins.

## Inputs

1. **RFP or funding announcement** — paste the full text, or paste the key sections
   (eligibility, priorities, award range, deadline). A URL alone is not enough; paste the
   content.
2. **Org profile** — a paragraph or bullet list covering: mission, primary geography,
   population served, annual budget, and any recent relevant projects or awards.

If either input is missing, ask for it before proceeding.

## Steps

1. **Extract funder priorities.** Pull 3–5 bullet points directly from the RFP stating what
   the funder cares most about. Quote their words, do not paraphrase.

2. **Score fit on three dimensions.** For each, give one of: Strong / Partial / Weak.
   - **Mission alignment** — does the org's work directly match the funder's stated priorities?
   - **Eligibility** — does the org meet every hard eligibility criterion (geography, type,
     budget size, prior funding restrictions)?
   - **Capacity** — does the org have the track record or staff to deliver what the funder
     expects at this award level?

3. **Issue a verdict.**
   - **High** — all three dimensions are Strong or at worst one Partial.
   - **Medium** — one or two dimensions are Partial with no Weak.
   - **Low** — any dimension is Weak, or eligibility is uncertain.

4. **For High:** list the 3 RFP requirements most likely to be underdeveloped in a first draft
   (the gaps that lose funded proposals, not the ones that are obvious).

5. **For Medium:** list 2–3 concrete actions the org can take before submitting to move from
   Partial to Strong on the weak dimensions.

6. **For Low:** state the specific blocker(s) clearly and directly. Do not soften them.

## Output

A structured fit memo with these sections in order:
- Funder priorities (bullets, their words)
- Dimension scores (table: Dimension | Score | One-line reason)
- Verdict (one word + one sentence)
- Next steps (High: watch-out list; Medium: strengthening actions; Low: blocker list)

Keep the whole memo under 400 words.

## Don't

- Do not write any proposal narrative or draft any grant sections.
- Do not estimate award probability as a percentage.
- Do not invent details about the org if they were not provided — ask instead.
- Do not give a High verdict when any eligibility criterion is unconfirmed.
- Do not use hedge phrases like "it depends" without immediately saying what it depends on.
